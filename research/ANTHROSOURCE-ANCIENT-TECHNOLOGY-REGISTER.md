# BIUPIU ANTHROSOURCE / ANCIENT TECHNOLOGY REGISTER

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 15 September 2026  
**Status:** Active — anthropology/archaeology evidence layer

## Purpose

AnthroSource is now a permanent Biupiu evidence source for Ancient Applied Technology, archaeology, anthropology, craft production, human-material relationships, cultural ecology, technology transmission and ancient agricultural landscapes. AnthroSource is the American Anthropological Association's searchable digital resource for AAA publications; AAA states that the service contains more than 250,000 articles and includes publications such as *American Anthropologist* and *Archaeological Papers of the AAA*.

Use AnthroSource to identify how technologies were embedded in human behaviour, craft practice, landscapes, institutions and ecological systems. Do not use an anthropology article as proof of an engineering performance claim that it does not directly test.

## Initial indexed records

### AS-AAT-001 — Agricultural landscapes and relict field systems
**Source:** Casana, J. (2024), “Chapter 1. The state of the field: Emerging approaches to the archaeology of agricultural landscapes,” *Archaeological Papers of the American Anthropological Association*. DOI: 10.1111/apaa.12181.  
**AnthroSource:** https://anthrosource.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/apaa.12181  
**Relevant divisions:** AAT, AGRI, GEOARCH, LAND-GIS, ALA, WATER, CODEX.  
**Use:** Supports landscape-scale study of ancient agricultural fields and the use of new methods to identify and interpret relict field systems. Treat as methodological/archaeological support, not as proof of a specific Biupiu agricultural technology.

### AS-AAT-002 — Technology as material and behavioural selection
**Source:** Bleed, P. (1997), “Content as Variability, Result as Selection: Toward a Behavioral Definition of Technology,” *Archaeological Papers of the American Anthropological Association*, 7(1), 95–104. DOI: 10.1525/ap3a.1997.7.1.95.  
**AnthroSource:** https://anthrosource.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1525/ap3a.1997.7.1.95  
**Relevant divisions:** AAT, AAT-H, MATERIALS, CODEX, IP.  
**Use:** Provides a framework for treating technology as involving both material and non-material/behavioural components. This supports Biupiu's rule that a technology record should include process, skill, selection, context and output rather than only the final artefact.

### AS-AAT-003 — Craft production, fibres and metals
**Source:** Hendon, J. A. (2015), “Producing Goods, Shaping People: The Materiality of Crafting,” *Archaeological Papers of the American Anthropological Association*, 26(1), 149–165. DOI: 10.1111/apaa.12068.  
**AnthroSource:** https://anthrosource.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/apaa.12068  
**Relevant divisions:** AAT, AAT-H, HEMP, TEXTILES, MATERIALS, BIOCARBON, COMPOSITES.  
**Use:** Links craft production to people, materials and social value, with examples involving Maya fibre work and Moche metalworking. Use as a bridge from ancient craft/process research to modern material and process engineering.

### AS-AAT-004 — Early blade technologies
**Source:** Bar-Yosef, O. (1999), “The Big Deal about Blades: Laminar Technologies and Human Evolution,” *American Anthropologist*, 101(2), 322–338. DOI: 10.1525/aa.1999.101.2.322.  
**AnthroSource:** https://anthrosource.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1525/aa.1999.101.2.322  
**Relevant divisions:** AAT, AAT-H, MATERIALS, GEOMETRY, PALAEO, CODEX.  
**Use:** Demonstrates that blade technologies have deeper and more complex chronologies than a simple Upper-Paleolithic association. Useful for tracing geometry/process evolution without assuming a single technological origin.

### AS-AAT-005 — Stone-tool making and materiality
**Source:** Efrati, B. (2026), “Reframing the Chipped Edge: Combining Materiality, Ontology, and Embodiment to Rethink Stone Tool-Making and Human Conscious Behavior in the Paleolithic Past,” *Anthropology of Consciousness*, 37, e70016. DOI: 10.1111/anoc.70016.  
**AnthroSource:** https://anthrosource.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/anoc.70016  
**Relevant divisions:** AAT, AAT-H, GEOMETRY, MATERIALS, PALAEO.  
**Use:** Contemporary anthropology perspective on stone-tool making and human-material interaction. Use to inform research questions about process and embodied skill, not to infer untested engineering mechanisms.

## Source-control rule

AnthroSource records must capture exact title, author, year, DOI/stable URL, relevant pages/section, proposition used, source lineage, department codes and whether a primary archaeological or scientific source should be preferred.

AnthroSource is complementary to JSTOR and primary archaeology. ResearchGate is a scientific/engineering discovery layer; Emerald is a systems/implementation layer; patents and public/declassified archives address engineering implementations and prior art; CODEX turns validated questions into reproducible computational tests.
