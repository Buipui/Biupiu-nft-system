## SR-71 / Blackbird Reference Layer

NASA documents the Blackbird family as including the A-12, YF-12, M-21, D-21 and SR-71. NASA's historical material describes the design evolution, high-temperature materials, aerodynamic heating, propulsion, structures and high-speed research conducted with the aircraft. citeturn0search3turn0search25

The SR-71 research layer therefore focuses on publicly documented design principles rather than attempting to reconstruct restricted engineering details.

### Indexed geometry themes

- highly slender fuselage geometry
- chine/leading-edge planform features
- engine nacelle integration
- twin vertical-tail arrangement
- highly swept lifting surfaces
- area distribution and wave-drag considerations
- inlet/engine integration as a historical aerodynamic study
- high-temperature structural/material constraints
- thermal expansion and high-speed skin behaviour
- aerodynamic heating
- stability and control
- high-speed/high-altitude flight envelope

NASA records the SR-71 at approximately Mach 3.2 and above 80,000 ft, with the airframe constructed largely from titanium and titanium alloys for the thermal environment. citeturn0search0turn0search4

NASA's NTRS paper *Design and Development of the Blackbird: Challenges and Lessons Learned* is a primary historical source for the design evolution from the Archangel/A-12 studies through the production SR-71. citeturn0search3

## Declassified Stealth-Craft Geometry Layer

The repository may cross-reference publicly declassified aircraft such as:

- Lockheed Have Blue / F-117 family
- Northrop Tacit Blue
- B-2 development history where public sources support the geometry discussion
- other officially released stealth demonstrators and technology demonstrators

The purpose is comparative geometry and engineering-history analysis, not operational replication.

The U.S. Air Force records that the F-117 was based on the Have Blue technology demonstrator and was the first operational aircraft designed around stealth technology. citeturn0search13

The National Museum of the U.S. Air Force records that Tacit Blue was developed in secrecy, later declassified, and demonstrated that stealth-compatible aircraft could use curved surfaces rather than relying exclusively on the faceted geometry associated with the F-117. citeturn0search12

## Geometry Research Questions

### STEALTH-GEO-01 — Faceted versus curved stealth geometry

Compare idealised faceted and curved surfaces using computational electromagnetic models while controlling dimensions, material assumptions and illumination conditions.

Primary outputs:

- radar-scattering proxy metrics
- angular response
- edge/corner contributions
- surface-current distributions
- sensitivity to geometric perturbations

This is a research model and must not be treated as a reconstruction of classified aircraft signatures.

### SR71-GEO-01 — High-speed planform analysis

Create a public-source-derived parametric representation of Blackbird-class geometry and investigate:

- slenderness
- sweep
- chine geometry
- planform area distribution
- inlet/nacelle placement
- control-surface arrangement

Outputs should be aerodynamic/geometric comparisons rather than operational performance predictions.

### SR71-AERO-02 — Thermal/aerodynamic geometry coupling

Investigate the relationship between high-speed aerodynamic loading, surface temperature and geometric/structural constraints using publicly documented assumptions.

### STEALTH-EM-01 — Electromagnetic geometry study

Use idealised stealth geometries to investigate how surface orientation, edges, curvature and discontinuities affect electromagnetic scattering.

### AERO-CG-01 — Computational aircraft geometry

Build parametric 3D aircraft forms suitable for CAD, CFD, visualisation and NFT artwork while maintaining a clear distinction between artistic reconstruction and engineering evidence.

- Historical aircraft configuration: **Established** where supported by official records.
- Aerodynamic principles: **Established / Supported Research** depending on the specific model.
- Reconstructed geometry: **Plausible Model** unless directly documented.
