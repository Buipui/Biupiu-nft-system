## Current mathematical directions worth routing into MATH

### AI-assisted formal mathematics
A July 2026 research position paper describes the shift from predefined theorem solvers toward research agents capable of decomposition, autoformalization and proof synthesis, while noting major limitations on open-ended research mathematics. Route: MATH + AI + COMPUTE. 

### Semantic theorem retrieval
An ICLR 2026 paper reports semantic search over 9 million mathematical theorems. Route: MATH + AI + COMPUTE. This suggests theorem retrieval should be a first-class service rather than ordinary keyword search.

### Multilingual problem retrieval
MathNet provides 30,676 expert-authored problems with solutions across 17 languages and 47 countries. Route: MATH + AI + COMPUTE + multilingual source layer.

### Formal proof robustness
MathAdv (2026) evaluates theorem proving across 13 domains and includes Lean 4 resources. Route: MATH + AI + FORMAL-VERIFICATION.

### Russian mathematics
Math-Net.Ru currently exposes 2026 results including second-order necessary optimality conditions for multistep Volterra-type control, stochastic two-level optimisation, and epsilon-optimality conditions for nonconvex optimal control. Route: MATH + CONTROL + ROBOTICS + AERO + DIGITAL-TWIN.

### OpenBooks
ITMO OpenBooks currently indexes more than 13,000 articles and exposes mathematical modelling, optimisation and algorithm material. Relevant records include optimisation of design decisions using AI in integrated CAD and real-time scheduling methods.

### Chinese-language research
A 2026 Chinese journal record covers mathematical foundations of AI, optimisation-based AI algorithms, intelligent computation/control and mathematical modelling for industry; another 2026 paper studies intelligent optimisation algorithms for nonconvex/nondifferentiable optimisation.

### Japanese-language engineering mathematics
A 2026 J-STAGE paper reports link-configuration optimisation for increasing machining-robot rigidity, including physical verification. Route: MATH + ROBOTICS + GEOMETRY + ADV-MFG.

## Cross-link to government/public engineering research

DARPA expMath focuses on decomposing difficult mathematics into reusable lemmas and autoformalization; DARPA DIAL focuses on discovering generalisable numerical algorithms. NASA's open-source catalogue includes Dantzig-Wolfe decomposition, while NASA NTRS reports multi-fidelity optimisation under uncertainty. NASA's Copernicus 5.4 release adds current trajectory-design capabilities including CR3BP propagation and Python API improvements.

These are research/engineering references, not evidence that any Biupiu implementation is equivalent.
