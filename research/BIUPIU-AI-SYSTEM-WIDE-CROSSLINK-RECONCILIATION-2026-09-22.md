## Native coding cross-reference
Every native AI change is evaluated against:
1. Requirement and owning authority.
2. Language and ABI boundary.
3. Contract/schema and version.
4. Source provenance and licence.
5. Security and fail-closed behaviour.
6. Dependency/provider classification.
7. Semantic/source audit.
8. Language and cross-language tests.
9. Regression and rollback evidence.
10. Runtime/device evidence.
11. Human promotion gate.

### Explicit semantic rules
- CODED != EXECUTED != RUNTIME VERIFIED != PHYSICALLY VERIFIED.
- Library/dependency presence never implies runtime capability.
- Unknown capability/provider states fail closed.
- Vendor SDKs and proprietary binaries remain outside the native core unless separately authorised and evidenced.
- External research/code is a reference or adapter until provenance, licence, security, build, regression and promotion gates pass.
- UI/framework integration cannot become OS authority.
- AI/ML proposals cannot bypass Core OS/DMS validation.
- Observed, computed, simulated, inferred and actuated states remain distinct.
- Learning may consume failure/regression evidence but cannot silently promote code.
- Cross-language interfaces require explicit ownership, lifetime, error, alignment, serialization, version and concurrency contracts.
