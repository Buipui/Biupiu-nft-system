# Biupiu Plant-Based Blade & Bio-Composite Materials Matrix v1.0

Date: 18 September 2026  
Status: Research / engineering qualification  
Scope: Biupiu blade micro-turbines and cross-department plant-material allocation

## Engineering conclusion
For Biupiu blade micro-turbines, **hemp and flax are the primary plant-fibre candidates** for structural composite skins and shear-load-bearing regions, subject to laminate-specific qualification. Current literature specifically identifies flax and hemp as promising natural-fibre options for wind-turbine blade structures when deployed in stiffness-driven biaxial plies. [RG-001]

For small turbine blades, hemp is also promising because of its combination of low density and useful tensile/shear performance. [RG-002]

**Boundary:** this does not establish flight, marine or high-temperature turbine certification. Natural fibres require dedicated qualification for moisture, temperature, fire, erosion, fatigue, lightning and manufacturing variability.

## Candidate hierarchy
| Candidate | Functional property | Biupiu blade role | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hemp fibre | specific stiffness/strength, low density | shell, biaxial skins, shear-web reinforcement, small-turbine blades | A |
| Flax fibre | high specific stiffness | shell skins, biaxial plies, vibration-sensitive structures | A |
| Bamboo | lightweight structural reinforcement | secondary skins, ducts, fairings | B |
| Kenaf | lightweight bast fibre | secondary laminates, ducts | B |
| Sisal | tensile strength / abrasion potential | toughened skins, protection | B |
| Jute | low-cost reinforcement | prototype blades, panels, fairings | B |
| Pineapple leaf fibre | strong leaf fibre | hybrid reinforcement / impact layers | B |
| Banana fibre | agricultural residue fibre | secondary composites | C |
| Coir | tough / impact-oriented | edge protection, damping | C |
| Rice husk / bagasse | bio-filler / residue | core fillers and resin modifiers | C |
| Cork | low density / damping | core and vibration layer | B |
| Bio-based resin | renewable matrix | qualified natural-fibre laminate matrix R&D | A |
| Basalt hybrid | high-load mineral reinforcement; not plant-based | critical-load hybrid zones | A |

## Recommended architecture
Concept: protective coating → hemp/flax biaxial skin → hemp/flax UD reinforcement → bio-resin → lightweight core → hemp/flax reinforcement → inner skin.

For higher-load versions, retain a hemp/flax laminate with basalt hybrid reinforcement in critical load paths. Exact fibre volume fraction, ply angle, resin, core and thickness must be determined by CFD/aeroelastic loads, FEA and physical qualification.

## Micro-turbine constraints
1. Centrifugal stress and fatigue must be modelled before selecting natural-fibre content.
2. Moisture uptake can alter interfaces and dimensions.
3. Conventional plant-fibre composites are not candidates for hot-gas turbine cores without a separately qualified high-temperature system.
4. Leading edges require erosion protection.
5. Aerospace/marine versions require dedicated fire/lightning/environmental qualification.
6. Fibre quality, retting, moisture and alignment require process control.
7. Every rotor requires mass-balance and resonance verification.
8. Vehicle airflow-harvesting concepts must measure net power against added aerodynamic drag.

## Evidence rule
These sources establish research candidates, not certification. All Biupiu material records must retain source, test conditions, matrix, fibre treatment, fibre orientation, volume/weight fraction, humidity, temperature, loading mode and failure mode.
