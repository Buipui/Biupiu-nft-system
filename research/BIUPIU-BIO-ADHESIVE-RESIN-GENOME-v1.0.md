## Research finding

The literature supports a dedicated matrix-development program rather than assuming that a plant fibre automatically becomes a sustainable composite. Bio-epoxy systems are being developed from plant oils, lignin, saccharides, tannins, cardanol, terpenes, rosins and related renewable feedstocks, with bio-based curing agents including polyamines, polyamides, amidoamines and cardanol-derived systems. citeturn0search2turn0search9

ResearchGate-indexed work also specifically investigates bio-filler epoxy composites for wind-turbine blades, while natural-fibre epoxy reviews identify matrix formulation, fibre treatment, curing and manufacturing as major performance variables. citeturn0search0turn0search4

A particularly relevant 2026 study reports amine-functionalised lignin used as a bio-based curing agent in epoxy resin and evaluates its use in structural adhesive and wind-turbine composite applications. This makes **lignin-derived curing chemistry a priority Biupiu research track**, not a claim that the reported formulation is already suitable for Biupiu blades. citeturn0search6

ResearchGate also indexes work using **foxtail-millet-husk biosilica + basalt fibre + epoxy** for wind-turbine blade applications, creating a useful route for Biupiu agricultural-residue-to-functional-filler research. citeturn0search8

## Adhesive families

### A. Structural bio-epoxy adhesive
Priority for blade bond-line research.

Targets:
- lap-shear strength
- mode-I fracture toughness
- mode-II fracture toughness
- peel resistance
- fatigue crack growth
- glass transition temperature
- water uptake
- thermal cycling
- bond-line thickness sensitivity

### B. Lignin-based adhesive
Priority for Biupiu's forestry/agricultural-carbon integration.

Research question:
Can lignin simultaneously contribute to resin chemistry, curing and carbon/biomass circularity without unacceptable loss of toughness or processability?

### C. Plant-oil adhesive
Priority for flexible bonding and secondary structures.

### D. Tannin / polyphenol adhesive
Priority for renewable bonding, coatings and potentially flame/thermal functionality.

### E. Rosin/cardanol adhesive
Priority for tack, flexibility, coatings and hybrid resin formulations.

## Fibre-to-matrix interface program

Natural-fibre performance is strongly affected by fibre treatment and fibre/matrix compatibility. The test matrix therefore includes:

- untreated fibre
- alkali-treated fibre
- silane/coupling-agent treatment
- bio-based surface treatment candidates
- controlled drying
- moisture-conditioned fibre
- plasma/physical surface modification where available

No treatment is assumed superior before testing. Impact literature specifically identifies fibre content, fibre length, stacking sequence, treatment, coupling agents, curing agents and manufacturing technique as important variables. citeturn0search3

## BM-02 qualification rule

A bio-content percentage alone does not qualify a resin as suitable for a structural blade.

A formulation advances only when its:
1. mechanical properties,
2. thermal properties,
3. moisture behaviour,
4. fatigue behaviour,
5. interface performance,
6. manufacturability,
7. ageing behaviour,
8. uncertainty bounds

are measured under defined conditions.
