# Biupiu World Civilisation Farming Sites v1.0

## Design directive

Biupiu World civilisation sites should visually and mechanically resemble their historical/environmental setting as closely as the evidence allows.

The Biupiu Civilisation Index + Search Protocol remains the governing research workflow: identify primary/archaeological evidence; cross-check reputable museum, university, archaeological and heritage sources; separate established evidence from reconstruction; mark uncertain/contested features; translate verified agricultural systems into simulation mechanics; preserve site-specific ecology, terrain, water and materials; document sources and confidence for major reconstructions.

The goal is historically informed simulation, not a claim that reconstructed virtual environments are exact archaeological replicas.

# 1. Inca Mountain Agricultural World

Build around steep Andean terrain, stone terraces, mountain sightlines, water channels, agricultural settlements and highland ecological zones. Reference forms include Machu Picchu/Pisac-style hillside terraces and Moray's concentric agricultural terraces. Smithsonian documentation describes Inka terracing as a major method for expanding agriculture across steep Andean terrain. citeturn0search2turn0search4

Moray becomes an Agricultural Research Zone. Its concentric terraces, irrigation channels and changing environmental conditions are suitable for crop experimentation. citeturn0search1turn0search13

Simulation variables: altitude, terrace construction, soil retention, water channels, crop zones, seed selection, labour, harvest storage, pack transport and inter-community barter.

Progression: Mountain settlement -> terrace construction -> irrigation -> planting -> harvest -> storage -> transport -> barter.

# 2. Maya Tropical Agricultural World

Build a tropical lowland environment with forest, limestone terrain, settlement zones, milpa fields, terraces where supported by evidence, and wetland agricultural zones where appropriate. Research identifies milpa cultivation as central to Maya agriculture and documents terrace networks and wetland raised-field systems. citeturn0search5turn0search11

Terracing should be geographically selective. Archaeological research shows terraces diverted/slowed runoff and helped build productive planting surfaces in particular Maya landscapes. citeturn0search6turn0search16

Simulation variables: forest clearance/regrowth, milpa cycles, soil fertility, rainfall, terraces, wetland raised fields, drainage, crop diversity and population pressure.

# 3. Babylonian / Mesopotamian Agricultural World

The Babylon site should be visually dominated by flat alluvial landscape, mud-brick architecture, canals, river-fed agriculture, irrigated fields, date palms, garden zones and monumental urban architecture.

## Hanging Gardens reconstruction rule

The Hanging Gardens must be treated as historically contested. Later classical accounts describe elevated terraces, trees, flowers, layered construction and mechanisms for raising water. However, no definitive archaeological evidence for the gardens has been found at Babylon, and Babylonian records do not securely document them. citeturn0search0turn0search3

Biupiu World therefore implements two layers: a Historical Babylon layer for evidence-grounded irrigation agriculture and gardens; and a clearly labelled Hanging Gardens Reconstruction/Interpretation Zone based on later accounts.

The reconstruction can model stepped terraces, deep planting beds, waterproofing layers, elevated water distribution, river/water-source logistics, tree/crop selection, maintenance labour and water-loss risk.

Later accounts associate the gardens with Nebuchadnezzar II, but this remains part of the historical reconstruction rather than a proven archaeological fact. citeturn0search3 An alternative Nineveh hypothesis may be presented as an optional research scenario and must not be silently merged into the Babylon historical map. citeturn0search7

# 4. Site-specific economics

| Site | Main farming mechanic | Environment |
|---|---|---|
| Inca | terraces + altitude + irrigation | Andes |
| Maya | milpa + selective terraces + wetlands | tropical lowlands |
| Babylon | canal irrigation + alluvial farming + gardens | Mesopotamian plain |
| Hanging Gardens | elevated garden irrigation reconstruction | contested reconstruction |

The same crop must not automatically have identical yield, water requirements or economic value at every site.

# 5. Barter

Each civilisation receives a local virtual economy. Inca: crop surplus -> storage -> transport -> mountain barter. Maya: maize/beans/squash surplus -> household/community exchange. Babylon: grain/date/garden production -> canal/urban exchange. These are educational simulations, not exact historical prices.

# 6. Historical accuracy engine

Every reconstructed asset should carry: civilisation ID, site ID, period, evidence class, source references, confidence status, reconstruction flag and simulation assumptions.

Evidence classes: PRIMARY, ARCHAEOLOGICAL, SCHOLARLY, SECONDARY, RECONSTRUCTION, CONTESTED.

A CONTESTED asset must not silently become historical fact in the user interface.

# 7. Visual rule

Do not homogenise civilisations into generic fantasy. Preserve local terrain, architecture, water-management technology, agricultural geometry, vegetation, materials, settlement pattern, transport systems and economic mechanics. Biupiu branding should remain subtle through interface elements and simulation systems rather than replacing historical visual language.

# 8. Subscriber learning loop

Explore site -> inspect evidence -> enter farm -> operate agricultural system -> harvest -> trade/barter -> manage treasury -> compare outcomes -> unlock research notes -> test alternative historical scenarios.

This makes Biupiu World a digital civilisation museum + farming simulator + economic laboratory + educational workshop.

## Accuracy status

- Inca terrace foundation: evidence-supported.
- Moray agricultural-experiment interpretation: supported but specific historical functions remain interpretive.
- Maya milpa/terrace/wetland diversity: evidence-supported.
- Babylonian irrigation agriculture: evidence-supported.
- Hanging Gardens: documented in later sources but archaeologically unresolved.
- Hanging Gardens appearance: reconstruction, not archaeological certainty.
